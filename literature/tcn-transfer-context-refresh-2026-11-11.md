# Mixed-provider TCN training and temporal context — November 11, 2026

## Decision and search scope

Question: after the registered expanded-data TCN regressed, which single intervention is most defensible before the November16 freeze? Targeted primary-source searches covered multi-domain imbalance, sampling versus loss weights, domain-adaptive pretraining, temporal segmentation context, and soccer trajectory/possession models. This is a focused evidence review, not a systematic review or an estimated winning score. Read project memory and the November10 research review. No tests, inference, training, data downloads, private match arrays, protected inputs or competition labels were accessed. No competition context was sent to sources.

**Recommendation:** first test ONE fixed provider-balanced sampler, with all current training settings frozen, followed conditionally by a separate longer-context trial. Treat source-pretraining/IDSSE adaptation as a third, separately budgeted possibility rather than immediately fine-tuning the all53 checkpoint. The project's measured 92.8–92.9% PFF training-window share makes sampling directly testable. It does not prove sampling caused failure. Confidence in prioritizing this diagnostic is moderate; confidence in an improvement, its magnitude or hackathon ranking is low.

## Evidence, quality and limits

### 1. Source mixture matters, but no paper establishes our correct ratio

[Yang, Wang and Katabi, ECCV2022, On Multi-Domain Long-Tailed Recognition, Imbalanced Domain Generalization and Beyond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09513) formalizes domain/class imbalance and evaluates BoDA on five curated multi-domain benchmarks against twenty algorithms. This is multi-benchmark computer-vision evidence that domains and label distributions interact; it does not validate a 50/50 provider sampler, football labels or this network. Balanced alignment/calibration is more than changing sampling. No author code is proposed for import.

[Salmani et al., 2025 preprint, Sampling and Loss Weights in Multi-Domain Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06913) distinguishes the effects of sampling probabilities and loss multipliers through linear-regression theory and empirical work. It supports treating them as different interventions. This is not peer-reviewed football evidence, and its linear setting cannot establish TCN optimizer behavior. Do not combine balancing and loss reweighting in the next trial or imply their equivalence.

Project evidence is more direct: the same fixed budget on the expanded pool lowered blend spellF1 from .592502/.428386 to .520562/.376959 clean/corrupted, with 0/5 paired wins. These are correlated reused-development results, not an independent test of why it failed. Both provider shift and lower IDSSE exposure changed; convergence was not isolated. A sampler comparison can narrow that uncertainty without architecture changes.

### 2. Adaptation is plausible, but generic pretraining papers do not prove a soccer benefit

[Gururangan et al., ACL2020, Don't Stop Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10964) evaluates four text domains and eight classification tasks. Additional domain/task-adaptive language-model pretraining improves downstream results across its settings. This is controlled multi-task evidence for adaptation in language; it is a distant analogy for supervised trajectory TCN fine-tuning, not direct evidence or an effect-size forecast. Its task-adaptation stage uses unlabeled text, whereas our possible trial would use IDSSE labels.

A material project constraint: the final53 checkpoint has seen **all five IDSSE development matches**. Fine-tuning it then scoring those matches would leak evaluation labels through initialization. Existing fold-specific expanded checkpoints exclude that fold's IDSSE match, but continuing them adds optimization budget. Any adaptation study must lock total source/IDSSE updates and provide an equal-budget comparator, reset or retain optimizer explicitly, preserve seed and mask separation, and start from fold-safe checkpoints or fresh fold-safe pretraining. Never rescue the failed all53 checkpoint with a nominal cross-validation wrapper. Fine-tuning can help, but neither valid inputs nor available labels guarantee positive transfer.

### 3. Longer context has supporting segmentation evidence, not a guaranteed gap solution

[Yi, Wen and Jiang, BMVC2021, ASFormer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08568) tests hierarchical local connectivity and attention/refinement on three public action-segmentation datasets. It addresses limited training data as well as long sequences; this is architectural evidence on daily activities, not football. [Author source licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ChinaYi/ASFormer/main/LICENSE), rechecked November11, is MIT. No code or checkpoint was adopted. A complete ASFormer replacement would change many factors at once.

[Bahrami, Francesca and Gall, ICCV2023, How Much Temporal Long-Term Context is Needed for Action Segmentation?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11358), [official paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Bahrami_How_Much_Temporal_Long-Term_Context_is_Needed_for_Action_Segmentation_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf), evaluates sparse long-context attention on three datasets: 50Salads, Breakfast and Assembly101. Context ablations support longer context in those tasks. Assembly101 results also show metric trade-offs: its method leads the compared F1/accuracy metrics while another method has higher edit score. Longer context is therefore not uniform improvement across all segment measures. No corresponding soccer spellF1 experiment exists here.

Our learned convolutional field is 17 encoded samples (roughly .68s), with gap age retaining longer past information as a scalar. A larger input window alone does not enlarge that field. Increasing dense-supported dilation while retaining raw masks is a plausible next structural experiment, with halo expanded to cover the actual field. Context must not cross halves or rely on true-label segment boundaries. It cannot recover an unknowable possession simply by seeing more absent ball frames.

### 4. Soccer papers are relevant, but their scores and weights are not interchangeable

[Borghesi et al., ESWA2023, Using Temporal Convolutional Networks to estimate ball possession in soccer games](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/2976470) is directly about H/A/dead states with trajectory embeddings and invariant aggregation. Its reported accuracy is not official full-match spellF1. Prior project full-text audit records a small selected split from confidential professional matches and different missingness handling; it cannot establish our ceiling.

[Capellera et al., ACCV2024, TranSPORTmer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17785), [institutional source](https://www.iri.upc.edu/publications/show/2921), evaluates a masked temporal/set-attention framework on one soccer and two basketball datasets, emphasizing trajectory forecasting, inference and imputation. Its state taxonomy includes passes, possessions, uncontrolled and out-of-play; this differs from our H/A/D target. Public checkpoint provenance/licence has not been re-established in this review, so no weights or implementation are recommended for adoption. The mechanism supports masked social/temporal context, not promised possession-spell gains.

[PathCRF, 2026 primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12080v1) uses player-pair paths and known-live segmentation on seven Sportec matches. Existing project audit identifies possible canonical checkpoint overlap with our development/sealed matches. Do not use such weights. Its event metrics are not official possession-spellF1. This study motivates structured relations, but the project's single relative-XY experiment has already failed: do not repeat it as though still untested.

## Ranked bounded experiments

1. **Fixed provider sampler:** choose provider with one preregistered probability (for example 50/50 as a diagnostic, not claimed optimal), then draw uniformly over that provider's valid known-window starts. Keep architecture, total20k updates, batch8, gap policy, folds, decoder and blend fixed. No class weights or ratio sweep. Compare to the current best eligible candidate under the unchanged gate; also describe the difference versus the rejected expanded result. Report both actual exposure and per-provider training loss if feasible without data-dependent checkpoint choice. A failure is retained and counts toward the authorized track stop rule.
2. **Longer receptive field:** on whichever parent is eligible after step1, change temporal context only; preserve sample budget and output loss core, document any required halo/input expansion and compute cost. Use dense-support dilation, strict half boundaries, masks and whole-chunk/batch equivalence. Pick the field before execution, not from development outcomes. Keep normalizer and training data policy fixed.
3. **Source-to-IDSSE supervised adaptation, conditional on time:** lock a source-pretraining/IDSSE-adaptation schedule under a fixed total update budget, with fold-safe initialization and an equal-budget control. Never use final53 initialization for held-out development scoring. This is less cleanly attributable and requires more plumbing, so not the first experiment. No external pretrained model is necessary.

Every scored trial remains provisional because five repeatedly reused development matches are a weak basis for model selection. Neither more frames, more model names, a training-fit score nor independent-looking windows overcome that limitation. Preserve the sealed evaluation once after freeze, original gate, no target-label tuning, CPU reproducibility, and time reserved for release checks. No literature justifies a numerical ceiling or a claim of reaching .8 or winning.
