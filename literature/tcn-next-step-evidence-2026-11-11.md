## Evidence and strength

**Symmetry averaging has a principled mechanism; football benefit remains unmeasured.** Lyle et al., *On the Benefits of Invariance in Neural Networks* (2020 preprint) analyzes augmentation versus feature averaging, deriving generalization statements for convex losses and showing that learned augmentation invariance can fail away from the training distribution. This is theoretical plus empirical neural-network evidence, not a possession trial. The verified abstract does not supply experiment sample counts, so no effect estimate or precision claim is extracted. Its convex-loss result does not guarantee improvement in the nonlinear Viterbi + spellF1 metric. Confidence: moderate rationale to test symmetry, low confidence in the direction of this project's gain. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00178).

Cohen and Welling, *Group Equivariant Convolutional Networks*, ICML2016, supplies architectural symmetry methods and evaluates CIFAR10 and rotated MNIST. Those image benchmarks support representation sharing under valid group actions, not a direct estimate for relabelled football tracking. No numerical benchmark result is imported. Our proposal averages outputs of existing weights instead of implementing G-convolutions. Confidence: strong algebraic basis for equivariance, indirect empirical support only. [Primary proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/cohenc16.html).

**Seed ensembles are credible but costlier.** Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel and Blundell, *Simple and Scalable Predictive Uncertainty Estimation using Deep Ensembles*, NeurIPS2017, studies predictive uncertainty from independently trained networks. That is evidence for model diversity as a general method, not proof that an extra seed improves spellF1 here or that a particular seed count is optimal. A seed ensemble needs new fold-safe fits, preserves each member's budget but increases total compute, and deserves separate registration. The verified landing page supplies no study-level sample counts; none are invented. [Primary proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/hash/9ef2ed4b7fd2c810847ffa5fa85bce38-Abstract.html).

**Segmentation losses are task-related but not the next default.** Abu Farha and Gall, *MS-TCN*, CVPR2019, evaluates temporal action segmentation on three benchmarks: 50Salads, GTEA and Breakfast. It combines classification with a smoothing objective aimed at over-segmentation. This makes temporal losses relevant, but results on video action features and different segment metrics cannot forecast official possession spellF1. The project's weak short-spell recall and existing decoder already impose temporal regularity: more smoothing could remove real short spells. Choosing a new loss coefficient would also add tuning burden. This is a future hypothesis if current-leader errors demonstrate excessive fragmentation, not a reason to transplant a paper's coefficient. [Primary proceedings](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Abu_Farha_MS-TCN_Multi-Stage_Temporal_Convolutional_Network_for_Action_Segmentation_CVPR_2019_paper.html).

Caveat: home advantage and provider conventions may make original role-specific priors informative; forcing symmetry can hurt. This trial is selected for cheap falsifiability and no retraining, not because literature predicts a large effect.
